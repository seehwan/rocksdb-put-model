Related Work

Compaction, Write Stalls, and LSM-based KV Stores

This document surveys representative research and practitioner guides on LSM-tree compaction and write stalls, and explains how each connects to our Phase‑B findings (deep-level compaction dominating bandwidth, L0 backlog, and controller slowdowns).

Foundations: LSM-Tree Cost and Deep-Level Dominance

O’Neil et al. The Log‑Structured Merge‑Tree (LSM‑tree). Acta Informatica, 33(4):351–385, 1996.

Establishes leveled/size-tiered merging and shows why most bytes accumulate in deeper levels. This implies deep‑level compactions (e.g., L2–L4 in our dataset) dominate I/O, matching our per‑minute per‑level IO charts.

Modeling and Optimizing LSM Costs

Dayan, Athanassoulis, Idreos. Monkey: Optimal Navigable Key‑Value Store. SIGMOD, 2017.
Dayan, Athanassoulis, Idreos. Dostoevsky: Better Space–Time Trade‑offs in LSM Trees. SIGMOD, 2018.
Dayan, Idreos. The LSM‑tree: A Survey. Foundations and Trends in Databases, 2022.

These works provide principled models for LSM trade‑offs (filters vs write/compaction cost) and synthesize how parameter choices (leveling ratio, filters) move work across levels. They predict deep‑level cost concentration, which our per‑minute/per‑level IO measurements (L2–L4 GB/min spikes) instantiate on production‑style logs.

Reducing Compaction Interference

WiscKey. Separating Keys from Values in SSD‑Conscious Storage. USENIX FAST, 2016.
HashKV. Efficient GC for KV Separation. USENIX ATC, 2018.
Titan. RocksDB KV‑Separation Plugin (TiKV). VLDB (Industrial), 2019.
PebblesDB. Fragmented LSM Trees. SOSP, 2017.
SlimDB. A Space‑Efficient KV Store for LSM. SIGMOD, 2017.

KV‑separation (WiscKey → HashKV → Titan) reduces bytes rewritten at deep levels by moving large values off the LSM. Fragmentation (PebblesDB) shrinks overlap and rewrite scope. Space‑focused designs (SlimDB) also indirectly reduce compaction pressure. Our results match the intuition: when deep‑level write volume (L2–L4) surges, stalls spike; reducing deep‑level bytes is therefore a direct lever on stall risk.

Scheduling, Adaptivity, and Production Engines

X‑Engine (Alibaba). An Optimized Storage Engine for Large‑Scale E‑commerce. SIGMOD, 2018.
Fluid/Adaptive LSM Trees. (e.g., Fluid LSM). SIGMOD, 2022.
SplinterDB. A Write‑Optimized Alternative to LSM. USENIX ATC, 2023.

Production systems shape compaction over time (scheduling, admission control) and across resources (threads, I/O budgets). Adaptive LSMs modulate tiered/leveled behavior to avoid pathological bursts. Alternatives (SplinterDB) change the rewrite profile entirely. Our analysis complements these by pinpointing minute‑scale windows where L0 risk rises under L2–L4 surges—evidence to guide when schedulers should protect L0 lanes.

Operational Guidance (RocksDB & LSM Systems)

  • RocksDB Wiki: Write Stalls; Compaction; Rate Limiter; Background Threads/Jobs.
  • Apache Cassandra: Leveled Compaction Strategy (LCS) Docs.

These sources document real‑world triggers (e.g., cf-l0-file-count-limit, memtable-limit, pending_compaction_bytes), and recommend rate limiting around the sustainable boundary (S_max) and provisioning compaction resources— the same mitigations our analysis supports.

Common Ground with Our Results

  • Deep-level dominance: Prior theory predicts, and we observe, L2–L4 compactions consuming the bulk of IO.
  • Foreground interference: Heavy deep‑level compactions delay L0→L1 cleanup, increasing L0‑file‑count stalls.
  • Peak‑aware control: Average below S_max is insufficient—bursty GB/min surges map to minute‑scale stall spikes.

What’s New in Our Analysis

Minute‑Level, Per‑Level Micro‑analysis

We reconstruct per‑minute per‑level IO (increases only, not cumulative) and compaction time (seconds/minute/level), producing dot‑plots that avoid long‑window averaging artifacts.

Concurrency vs Stall: Direct Test

We quantify that raw compaction concurrency has weak linear correlation with stall time, while deep‑level IO volume strongly co‑varies with stall duration—sharpening the causal picture around where compaction work matters.

Quantitative Thresholding

For minutes with L2–L4 > 5 GB/min, average stall ≈ 51.4s; for ≤ 5 GB/min, ≈ 26.3s. Peak minutes (e.g., minute 874, 8.3 GB/min) reach ≈ 55.8s stall—turning qualitative guidance into reproducible numbers tied to the log.
